## Context

`Contract` today (`backend/app/models.py`) is a single table: `id`, `customer_location_id` (FK), `start_date`, `interval_days`, `duration_minutes`, plus `required_skills` via `contract_skills`. `ServiceVisit.contract_id` points at it directly, and every consumer of a visit's duration/skills/location reads through `visit.contract.*`: `backend/app/main.py` (`list_contracts`, `list_service_visits`, `list_assignments`, `create_assignment`, `propose_optimization`, `apply_optimization`), `backend/app/solver_client.py` (`_visit_payload`, `_existing_assignment_payload`), and the frontend's `UnassignedVisitList.tsx`, `AssignedVisitList.tsx`, `DayPlanningView.tsx`, `OptimizeView.tsx`, and the Customer Portal's `ContractsView.tsx`/`SkillsView.tsx`. This data is fully local — unlike `Customer`/`CustomerLocation`, nothing here is Tripletex-sourced. See proposal.md - Why for motivation; the three confirmed decisions (bare `Contract`, no visit-generation automation, cascade soft-delete) are treated as settled here, not re-litigated.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Every current reader of `visit.contract.duration_minutes` / `.required_skills` / `.customer_location` keeps working with an equivalent `visit.contract_line.*` path — this is a rename-and-relocate of an existing data shape, not a new one.
- Give the Customer Portal exactly enough write capability to manage `Contract`/`ContractLine` — nothing else in the portal becomes writable.

**Non-Goals:**
- No automatic `ServiceVisit` generation from a `ContractLine`'s schedule (confirmed) — `start_date`/`end_date`/`interval_days`/`duration_minutes` remain descriptive data; visits are still created the same way they are today (seed script, or manually).
- No cascading effect on already-generated `ServiceVisit`s when their `ContractLine` (or its parent `Contract`) is soft-deleted (confirmed) — matches how deleting a `Customer`/`CustomerLocation` already never hides its dependents.
- No authentication/authorization on the new write endpoints, consistent with the rest of the app.
- No top-level "Contract Lines" list view in the Customer Portal — lines are managed nested inside their parent Contract's detail view, matching how a contract line is inherently scoped to one contract; adding a 7th top-level nav entry for a nested concept would just duplicate what the Contract detail view already needs to show.

## Decisions

- **`Contract` becomes `id`, `customer_id`, `delete_flag` — nothing else** (confirmed). It's a bare grouping entity; every schedule/skill/location field moves to `ContractLine`.
- **Migration truncates and rebuilds, matching the Tripletex customer/location migrations' precedent**, rather than attempting a data-preserving transform (e.g. deriving each new `Contract.customer_id` from its old `customer_location.customer_id`, splitting each old contract row into a new contract + one line, remapping `contract_skills` and `service_visits.contract_id`). This is dev/demo-stage data that already gets rebuilt by the seed script after every prior structural migration; writing one-off data-migration logic for it would be real effort spent on rows nobody needs to keep. Migration truncates `assignments`, `service_visits`, `contract_skills`, `contracts`, drops the old `contract_skills` table, creates `contracts` fresh (slim), and creates `contract_lines` + `contract_line_skills`.
- **New endpoints are resource-scoped, not nested-only**: `POST /contracts`, `PATCH /contracts/{id}`, `DELETE /contracts/{id}` (soft) for the contract itself; `POST /contracts/{id}/lines` to create a line under a specific contract (so the backend can validate the chosen customer location belongs to that contract's customer), then `PATCH /contract-lines/{id}` and `DELETE /contract-lines/{id}` (soft) for an existing line directly (no need to re-specify the parent contract to edit or delete a line that already has one). No bare `GET /contract-lines` — a contract's lines are read through `GET /contracts` (each contract embeds its lines), matching the Non-Goal of no standalone Contract Lines list view.
- **Creating a contract line validates that the chosen `customer_location_id` belongs to the contract's own `customer_id`** — otherwise a line could reference a location for a completely different customer than its parent contract, which would make the Customer → Contract → ContractLine → CustomerLocation chain internally inconsistent. Rejected (422) if it doesn't match.
- **Soft-delete cascade is implemented in the `DELETE /contracts/{id}` handler itself** (mark the contract deleted, then mark every non-deleted line under it deleted too), not a database trigger — consistent with how every other soft-delete in this app (`Customer`, `CustomerLocation`) is an application-level flag flip, not a DB-level cascade.
- **`ServiceVisit.contract_id` is renamed to `contract_line_id`.** Every joinedload chain in `main.py` and every payload builder in `solver_client.py` that currently does `ServiceVisit.contract` / `Contract.customer_location` / `Contract.required_skills` is updated to `ServiceVisit.contract_line` / `ContractLine.customer_location` / `ContractLine.required_skills`. `ContractOut`/`ServiceVisitOut` in `schemas.py` gain a new `ContractLineOut` (mirroring today's `ContractOut` shape almost exactly, plus `end_date` and `delete_flag`), and `ServiceVisitOut.contract` becomes `ServiceVisitOut.contract_line: ContractLineOut`.
- **Frontend**: `types.ts`'s `ServiceVisit.contract: Contract` becomes `ServiceVisit.contract_line: ContractLine`, and `Contract` gains a `lines: ContractLine[]` field for the portal. Every component reading `visit.contract.*` (`UnassignedVisitList.tsx`, `AssignedVisitList.tsx`, `DayPlanningView.tsx`, `OptimizeView.tsx`) is updated to `visit.contract_line.*` — a pure rename at each call site, no behavior change. `ContractsView.tsx` gains real forms (create contract, create/edit/soft-delete line) since it's the one view moving from read-only to writable; `SkillsView.tsx`'s contract list becomes a contract-line list.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Truncating `contracts`/`service_visits`/`assignments` again destroys whatever demo scheduling data exists at migration time, including the just-synced-from-Tripletex customer locations' contracts] → Same accepted trade-off as the prior two Tripletex migrations; seed data is rebuilt fresh afterward per the Migration Plan below.
- [The Customer Portal's first write feature has no confirmation dialog for soft-delete, matching this app's existing no-confirmation-dialogs convention elsewhere] → Accepted, consistent with the rest of the app; soft-delete is non-destructive and reversible only by a future "undelete" action if one is ever added (not in this change).
- [A `ContractLine`'s `end_date` is pure metadata with nothing reading it yet, since visit generation is out of scope] → Accepted per the confirmed decision; it's there for a future scheduler to use, not dead weight forever, just unused today.

## Migration Plan

1. Add `ContractLine` (mirroring today's `Contract` fields, plus `end_date` and `delete_flag`) and `contract_line_skills` to `backend/app/models.py`; slim `Contract` down to `id`/`customer_id`/`delete_flag` with a `customer` relationship and a `lines` relationship; add `Customer.contracts`; change `ServiceVisit.contract_id` to `contract_line_id` and its relationship to `ContractLine`.
2. Migration: truncate `assignments`, `service_visits`, `contract_skills`, `contracts`; drop `contract_skills`; recreate `contracts` with the slim shape; create `contract_lines` and `contract_line_skills`; add `service_visits.contract_line_id`, drop `service_visits.contract_id`. Downgrade reverses all of it (best-effort, since the old per-location contract shape can't be reconstructed from post-split data — matches how the Tripletex migrations' downgrades already don't restore truncated demo rows either).
3. Update `backend/app/schemas.py`: add `ContractLineOut` (id, contract_id, customer_location, start_date, end_date, interval_days, duration_minutes, required_skills); slim `ContractOut` to id/customer/lines; `ServiceVisitOut.contract` → `ServiceVisitOut.contract_line: ContractLineOut`. Add `ContractCreate`/`ContractUpdate` and `ContractLineCreate`/`ContractLineUpdate` request schemas.
4. Update every `main.py` joinedload chain and field access enumerated in Context (list_contracts, list_service_visits, list_assignments, create_assignment, propose_optimization, apply_optimization) from `Contract`/`.contract` to `ContractLine`/`.contract_line`.
5. Add `POST /contracts`, `PATCH /contracts/{id}`, `DELETE /contracts/{id}`, `POST /contracts/{id}/lines`, `PATCH /contract-lines/{id}`, `DELETE /contract-lines/{id}` to `main.py`, including the cascade-on-contract-delete and the customer-location-belongs-to-contract's-customer validation.
6. Update `backend/app/solver_client.py`'s `_visit_payload` and `_existing_assignment_payload` from `visit.contract.*` to `visit.contract_line.*`.
7. Update `backend/app/seed.py`: one `Contract` per customer, one `ContractLine` per that customer's location (replacing today's one-contract-per-location fixtures), `ServiceVisit` generation pointed at the new lines.
8. Frontend: `types.ts` (`ServiceVisit.contract_line`, `Contract.lines`, new `ContractLine`/create-update payload types), `api.ts` (contract/contract-line CRUD calls), `UnassignedVisitList.tsx`/`AssignedVisitList.tsx`/`DayPlanningView.tsx`/`OptimizeView.tsx` (rename call sites), `ContractsView.tsx` (create/update/soft-delete forms for contracts and lines), `SkillsView.tsx` (contract lines instead of contracts).
9. Verify: a contract line's visit still shows correct customer/address/region/duration/skills everywhere it did before (Manual Assignment, Day Planning, Optimize, Customer Portal); creating/updating/soft-deleting a contract and a contract line works end-to-end through the Customer Portal; soft-deleting a contract cascades to its lines; a line under a soft-deleted (or any) contract only accepts a customer location belonging to that contract's customer; existing service visits are unaffected by soft-deleting their contract line; every other Customer Portal entity remains read-only.
10. Rollback: revert the migration (best-effort per step 2) and the application-code changes; this is a structural, not additive, change, so rollback means reverting to the pre-split shape entirely rather than toggling a flag.
